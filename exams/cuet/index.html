CUET-UG: Everything you should know

Next CUET-UG exam

Date to be announced by NTA.

A live countdown appears here as soon as the exam date is published.

CUET-UG, the Common University Entrance Test (UG), is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) for admission to undergraduate programmes of the University of Delhi, BHU, JNU, Allahabad University and many other central, state and private universities. Mathematics is one of its domain subjects.

ParticularOfficial Details

Question Paper Languages13 Languages: English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu
Marking Scheme
  • +5 marks for every correct answer
  • −1 mark negative marking for every wrong answer
  • 0 marks for unattempted questions
Syllabus CoveredStrictly Class 12 NCERT Syllabus (Domain subjects) + Reading Comprehension/Grammar (Languages) + Quantitative/Logical Reasoning & GK (General Test)
Age Limit & EligibilityNo age limit. Candidates who have passed or are appearing in Class 12 (10+2) are eligible (each university sets its own subject mapping & minimum pass criteria).

Section 2Subject Structure, Question Counts & Marking Pattern

Under the standardized NTA pattern, candidates can choose up to 5 subjects across three sections (Languages, Domain-Specific Subjects, and General Aptitude Test). Every single paper has 50 compulsory Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) to be solved in 60 minutes.

Three-Section Paper Structure (50 Compulsory Questions per Subject)

SectionAvailable SubjectsQuestions / PaperDurationMax Marks
Section I: Languages13 Languages (English, Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayalam, Odia, Punjabi, Assamese, Urdu)50 MCQs (All Compulsory)60 Mins250 Marks
Section II: Domain Subjects23 Class 12 Subjects: Mathematics/Applied Maths, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Computer Science/IP, Accountancy, Economics, Business Studies, History, Political Science, Geography, Psychology, Sociology, etc.50 MCQs (All Compulsory)60 Mins250 Marks
Section III: General Aptitude Test (GAT)General Knowledge, Current Affairs, General Mental Ability, Numerical Ability, Quantitative Reasoning (Class 8–10 level), Logical & Analytical Reasoning50 MCQs (All Compulsory)60 Mins250 Marks
Maximum 5- Subject TotalAny combination of up to 5 subjects (e.g., 1 Language + 3 Domains + 1 General Test, or 1 Language + 4 Domains)250 MCQs Total5 Hours (Across Shifts)1,250 Marks

Section 4Step-by-Step Timeline from Application to College Admission

StepStageWhat the Student Must Do
1Check University Mapping & Apply (Feb–Mar)Verify exact subject requirements of your target universities (e.g., DU requires subjects studied in Class 12), fill the NTA form on cuet.nta.nic.in, and pay the fee.
2City Slip, Admit Card & Exam (May–June)Download your City Intimation Slip and Admit Card, then appear for your chosen 60-minute CBT papers across scheduled shifts.
3Answer Key & NTA Scorecard (July)NTA releases provisional answer keys for challenge, followed by the final scorecard showing subject-wise Normalized Marks (out of 250) and Percentiles.
4Separate University Portal Registration (July–Aug)Crucial Step: Register separately on each target university's counselling portal (e.g., DU CSAS Portal, BHU CAP Portal) using your CUET application number.
5Preference Filling & Seat Allotment (Aug–Sept)Arrange college + course combinations in priority order, accept your allotted seat across rounds, complete online document verification, and pay the college fee.

Section 5Safe scores and 10-year marks vs rank

Normalized scores determine admission to central institutions like the University of Delhi (DU), BHU, JNU, and Allahabad University. The primary competitive benchmark evaluates an 800-mark scale (1 Language + 3 Domain Subjects @ 200 marks each) for Commerce and Humanities, and a 600-mark PCM/PCB aggregate for Sciences. Because each subject is normalized across multiple shifts, raw scores can convert differently depending on shift difficulty; high percentiles in domain subjects are critical for top North Campus programmes.

Section 8Seat Matrix & Reservation Rules (Central Universities)

All Central Universities (DU, BHU, JNU, Jamia, AU, etc.) follow the Government of India reservation policy. There is NO state domicile quota in Central Universities—students from every state compete equally.
